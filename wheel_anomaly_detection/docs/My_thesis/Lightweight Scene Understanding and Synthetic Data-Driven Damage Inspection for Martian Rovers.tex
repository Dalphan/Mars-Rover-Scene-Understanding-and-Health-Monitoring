\begin{abstract}
	This thesis investigates two applications for Martian rover autonomy: lightweight semantic scene understanding for navigation support and automated inspection of visible wheel damage. Segmentation architectures are evaluated on S5Mars, together with quantization strategies to reduce inference cost. SegFormer-B0 reaches 76.93\% test mean Intersection over Union after Quantization-Aware Training, while DeepLabV3+ with MobileNetV2 requires the least measured GPU energy per image. Separate MarsSeg experiments show better cross-mission transfer for SegFormer-B0 than for the tested convolutional models.

	For damage inspection, no suitable real-image benchmark with perforation masks was identified. An automated Blender pipeline therefore generates 10,000 annotated images of a Curiosity model, including matched normal and perforated wheel observations. Synthetic generation avoids extensive manual labeling while providing automatic ground truth and controlled variation of damage and imaging conditions. Four anomaly detection families are evaluated using only normal renders for training. On the overhead-view test subset, PatchCore Large achieves an image-level area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 86.43\%, whereas a compact TinyGLASS configuration has the lowest measured inference cost. Detection and localization results show that better image discrimination does not necessarily imply more precise damage maps. The findings provide an experimental basis for terrain interpretation and targeted wheel inspection, while reliable deployment requires real-image validation, adaptation across the synthetic-to-real gap, and measurements on representative hardware. Future integration could use the predicted \emph{Rover} region to guide a dedicated wheel localizer before applying the damage detector, combining navigation support and targeted inspection within a common onboard perception pipeline.
\end{abstract}
